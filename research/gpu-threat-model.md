# Threat model — `sparq-gpu` (deferral record + pre-model)

**Status: the deferral HOLDS for the runtime/device model, and is PARTIALLY LIFTED for the
supply-chain and build-isolation surface.**

[`research/threat-model.md`](threat-model.md) (the STABLE core) excludes `sparq-gpu` with the
row *"`publish = false`; measure-first PCIe-break-even prototype — bead **sq-vrye** (DEFER until
it exits the prototype stage)"*. This record is the companion that row points at. It exists so
the deferral is a **stated, checkable decision** rather than a blank space: it records what was
verified, what is genuinely deferrable, what is *not*, and the mechanical trigger that
discharges the rest.

Evidence-based, not aspirational, in the house style of the parent model: a mitigation is cited
by the code or test that actually establishes it, and a gap is stated plainly. Do not read a
cited mitigation as more than what its citation proves.

## 1. Premise check

The bead's premise — that `sparq-gpu` is still a prototype — is **confirmed**, and nothing here
argues for lifting the runtime deferral:

- The measured verdict is **PARK**, with re-open conditions, in
  [`research/gpu-verdict.md`](gpu-verdict.md) §5. No re-open condition has been met.
- `publish = false` is still set (`crates/sparq-gpu/Cargo.toml:15`).
- **Nothing in the workspace depends on it.** Verified directly: `sparq-gpu` appears in no other
  crate's `Cargo.toml`. There is no engine coupling, no scheduler integration, no residency
  cache, and no SPARQL-level routing of work to a device.
- The crate is `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` (`crates/sparq-gpu/src/lib.rs:33`), so the parent
  model's highest-severity asset (memory-safety of `unsafe` paths) has no host-side exposure
  here at all.

What the premise did **not** anticipate is the one thing that is true of a parked crate that is
still a **workspace member**: it is absent from the shipped artifacts but present in the
resolved lockfile, and therefore present in the workspace's supply-chain posture. That is the
part of the deferral this record lifts. Everything that requires a *running device driving real
data* stays deferred.

## 2. Scope and posture

**In scope of this record:** `crates/sparq-gpu` as it exists today — four WGSL compute kernels
(FILTER u32, FILTER f64, hash-join probe, GROUP BY COUNT+SUM), their host driver, the scalar CPU
reference oracles in `src/cpu.rs`, the differential tests, and the benchmark example.

**Posture.** The crate is a **measurement instrument**, not a product surface. It is driven only
by its own tests and its own `examples/gpu_bench.rs`; its inputs are generated by those callers,
never by a query, never by an HTTP client, never by parsed RDF. **There is no attacker-controlled
path to any kernel today.** That is the single fact that makes the runtime deferral correct, and
it is also the single fact that stops being true the moment the crate is wired in — which is why
§5 catalogues what activates, and §6 defines the trigger.

## 3. Assets

Inherited from the parent model, re-prioritised for what this crate could actually touch:

1. **Query-result integrity** — that a device-computed answer equals the CPU answer. For this
   crate specifically, CPU/GPU result equivalence *is* the product; a silent divergence is the
   sharpest possible defect (§4.3).
2. **Developer and CI host integrity** — building or running this crate executes third-party
   build/FFI code and loads the host's native GPU driver (§4.1).
3. **Build-artifact isolation** — that the GPU stack never reaches a shipped bundle, above all
   a wasm bundle (§4.2).
4. **Availability** — that a kernel cannot wedge the device or block the host thread forever
   (§4.4).

Dataset confidentiality is **not** an asset of this crate today (no dataset ever reaches a
device buffer); it becomes the dominant asset on exit — see §5.2.

## 4. Threats that are LIVE today

### 4.1 T-GPU-SUP (Tampering / Elevation) — the GPU closure is in the workspace vet surface

`sparq-gpu` is a workspace member (`Cargo.toml` `members`), so its dependencies resolve into the
shared `Cargo.lock` that `cargo-deny` and `cargo-vet` are run against workspace-wide
(`.github/workflows/supply-chain.yml`).

Computed from the checked-in `Cargo.lock` by reachability from `sparq-gpu`, minus the union of
the closures of every other workspace member:

| measure | count |
|---|--:|
| packages reachable from `sparq-gpu` | 171 |
| packages reachable **only** via `sparq-gpu` | 55 |
| of those, unvetted `[[exemptions.*]]` in `supply-chain/config.toml` | 40 |

That is 40 of the repo's 494 cargo-vet exemptions — roughly 8% of the unvetted surface —
carried for a crate that ships in nothing. The closure includes crates whose whole purpose is
loading and talking to native graphics drivers (`libloading`, `khronos-egl`, `ash`, `wgpu-hal`,
`glow`) plus a shader-compiler front end (`naga`, `spirv`).

**Honest bound on severity.** No shipped binary, library, or wasm bundle links any of these, so
this is **not** an end-user exposure. What it is: (a) a developer/CI-machine exposure for anyone
who runs `cargo test -p sparq-gpu` or the bench example, whose trust boundary reaches all the way
out to the host's closed-source vendor driver; and (b) a standing tax on the repo's supply-chain
gates, where an advisory in any of the 55 lands on `main` for a parked prototype.

**Not mitigated, and not obviously worth mitigating** — moving the crate out of the workspace
would trade this for a second lockfile and a crate that silently rots. Recorded here as an
accepted, quantified cost, to be re-decided at exit (§7 P6).

### 4.2 T-GPU-ISO (Spoofing of a documented invariant) — "wgpu never enters the wasm build" is asserted three times and gated zero times

The isolation invariant is stated as a hard requirement in three places, one of which even
writes out the exact check:

- `crates/sparq-gpu/Cargo.toml:19-21` — *"`wgpu` must never enter the `sparq-wasm` dependency
  graph (CI builds `sparq-wasm` for wasm32-unknown-unknown; `cargo tree -p sparq-wasm
  --target wasm32-unknown-unknown | grep wgpu` must stay empty)"*
- `crates/sparq-gpu/README.md:16-17` — *"`wgpu` never enters the wasm build"*
- `skills/gpu-kernels/SKILL.md:26-29` — same claim, same `cargo tree` formulation

There is a gate that does exactly this shape of check — `scripts/wasm-deps-guard.sh`, which
inverts each shipped bundle's wasm32 dependency graph and fails on a forbidden crate. Its
forbidden set is:

```text
FORBIDDEN=(rayon flate2 zstd zstd-safe bzip2 sparq-parse mio tokio)
```

`wgpu` is **not in it**, and neither is `naga`, `wgpu-core`, or `wgpu-hal`
(`scripts/wasm-deps-guard.sh:36`). A repo-wide search finds no other CI step, script, or workflow
that checks for `wgpu` anywhere.

So the invariant is **currently true but vacuously so** — it holds because nothing depends on
`sparq-gpu` at all, not because anything enforces it. The claim in the three docs asserts an
enforcement that does not exist. A future dependency edge (the obvious one being verdict §5's
re-open condition 3, *WebGPU in the browser*, which would deliberately pull `wgpu` toward a wasm
target) would ship a native-driver-loading closure into a bundle graph with no gate firing and
no reviewer prompt.

**This is the one finding in this record with a cheap, complete fix**: add `wgpu naga wgpu-core
wgpu-hal` to that `FORBIDDEN` array. It is a one-line change that converts three prose assertions
into an enforced gate. It is deliberately **not** made in this record (design-only), and is
filed as §7 P1.

### 4.3 T-GPU-INT (Information disclosure via wrong answer) — CPU and GPU diverge on out-of-range GROUP BY keys, silently on the GPU side

`Gpu::group_aggregate` validates `1 <= groups <= MAX_GROUPS` and `keys.len() == vals.len()`
(`crates/sparq-gpu/src/lib.rs:535-539`) but **not** the documented precondition `keys[i] <
groups` — reasonably so, since an O(n) host scan would defeat the point of offloading. On a
malformed key the two sides then behave differently:

| side | behaviour on `k >= groups` | citation |
|---|---|---|
| CPU oracle | **panics** — `out[k as usize]` is an out-of-bounds index | `src/cpu.rs:83` |
| GPU kernel | accumulates into workgroup slot `k`; for `groups <= k < 512` that slot is **never flushed** to the output, so the row silently vanishes from the result | `src/lib.rs:266-268` vs the `g < params.g` flush loop at `src/lib.rs:271-283` |

For a crate whose entire deliverable is *"the GPU result equals the CPU result"*, a
CPU-fails-loudly / GPU-quietly-undercounts divergence is the sharpest correctness defect
available. It is **not caught** by any test: every differential test generates keys in range
(`tests/correctness.rs:157` uses `% groups`, and the randomized sweep at
`tests/correctness.rs:262` uses `rng.below(groups)`), so the divergent input is never produced.

Not exploitable today (no attacker reaches the API). It becomes a wrong-answer channel the moment
engine-derived keys reach the kernel, and — more immediately — it means the crate's central
equivalence claim is unverified at its most interesting boundary. Filed as §7 P3.

### 4.4 T-GPU-DoS (Denial of service) — an unbounded shader loop plus an unbounded host wait

The probe kernel walks the open-addressing table with an **unbounded** `loop` whose only exit is
finding an `EMPTY_KEY` slot (`crates/sparq-gpu/src/lib.rs:202-213`). Termination therefore
depends entirely on the table containing at least one free slot.

`cpu::build_hash_table` guarantees that — capacity is the next power of two `>= 2 x keys`, so
load factor stays `<= 0.5` (`src/cpu.rs:36-53`). But `Gpu::upload_table` accepts **any**
caller-supplied slice and checks only the power-of-two shape, via `debug_assert!`
(`src/lib.rs:431`) — which is compiled out in `--release`, the only profile the benchmark runs
in (`research/gpu-verdict.md` §6). `write_table` checks only the length
(`src/lib.rs:452-456`).

If a fully-occupied (or wrong-shaped) table reaches the device, the shader does not terminate,
and the host side then blocks forever: `run()` waits on
`wgpu::PollType::wait_indefinitely()` with no timeout (`src/lib.rs:650`). The realistic outcome
is a driver watchdog / TDR reset of the device, with a hung host thread.

Every in-tree caller builds its table with `cpu::build_hash_table`, so this is **latent, not
live**. It is recorded because a latent precondition guarded only by a `debug_assert!` is exactly
what stops being latent when a second caller appears. Filed as §7 P3/P4.

### 4.5 T-GPU-AVAIL-minor — unchecked buffer sizing and a truncating length cast

Two smaller latent issues, recorded for completeness rather than urgency:

- `Gpu` exposes `max_storage_bytes` (`src/lib.rs:343`) but **no upload path consults it** — the
  size check lives in the *test* (`tests/correctness.rs:194`), not the library. An oversized
  column therefore surfaces as a wgpu validation error/panic instead of a typed refusal.
- `upload_u32`/`upload_f64` store `data.len() as u32` (`src/lib.rs:418`, `:425`), a truncating
  cast. Bounded in practice — a column with more than `u32::MAX` elements would fail buffer
  allocation first — so this is a latent sharp edge, not a reachable defect.

## 5. Threats that are DEFERRED (they activate on exit)

These are **not** modelled here, and this record makes no claim about them. They are catalogued
so that the exit work in §6 is scoped rather than open-ended.

### 5.1 The vendor driver joins the query TCB

Wiring a device path in means a WGSL kernel is compiled by `naga` to SPIR-V/MSL/DXIL and then by
a closed-source vendor driver, and the result of that pipeline decides query answers. The driver
is outside every gate sparq has. Needs: a stance on driver/adapter allow-listing, a stance on
whether device results are cross-checked against the CPU, and a compiled-shader-cache trust
decision (`cache: None` today, `src/lib.rs:397`).

### 5.2 Residency, multi-tenancy, and device-memory remanence

Dataset confidentiality becomes the dominant asset the moment real columns are resident. Fresh
result buffers are spec-guaranteed zero-initialised (`src/lib.rs:576` and its comment), but
column buffers live for the lifetime of the `ColU32`/`ColF64` and are **not** explicitly zeroized
on drop. On a shared or multi-tenant GPU this is the central question, and it is precisely the
question a residency cache — verdict §5 re-open condition 2 — forces. Unassessed.

### 5.3 Timing and contention side-channels

Dispatch timing over resident data as an oracle; cross-tenant contention on a shared device.
Unassessed, and note that the verdict's own methodology section documents how strongly host
contention perturbs these timings (`research/gpu-verdict.md` §2.1) — a signal that the channel
is at least noisy.

### 5.4 Budget propagation

`sparq-engine`'s `QueryBudget` and its `CancelPtr` cancellation sites (cited in the parent model)
**do not reach the device**. A dispatched kernel runs to completion regardless. Any wiring-in
must answer how a cancelled or budget-exceeded query stops work already on the GPU.

### 5.5 The browser/WebGPU variant is a different model entirely

Verdict §5 re-open condition 3 (WebGPU as the only in-browser parallelism) puts the kernels in a
page's origin, against an adapter the page does not control, under a different set of limits.
That is its own threat model, not an extension of this one — and it is the path that would most
directly collide with §4.2.

### 5.6 Supply chain moves from dev-only to shipped

At exit, §4.1's 55-package closure stops being a developer-machine cost and becomes part of a
shipped artifact's attack surface, with the cargo-vet exemptions needing to be actually vetted.

## 6. Exit criteria — what discharges the rest of this deferral

"Exits the PCIe-break-even prototype stage" is currently a judgement call, which is why the bead
has sat open. Making it mechanical: **any one** of the following fires the trigger, and §5 must
be turned into a full model *before* that change merges.

1. Any workspace crate declares a dependency on `sparq-gpu`.
2. `publish = false` is removed from `crates/sparq-gpu/Cargo.toml`.
3. `wgpu`, `naga`, or any of their family appears in a shipped bundle's dependency graph
   (native or wasm).
4. A kernel is invoked with data derived from a query, a parsed RDF document, or any other
   input sparq did not generate itself.

Conditions 1 and 3 become *automatically detectable* once §7 P1 and P2 land, which is the main
argument for doing those two first: they turn this deferral from something a human has to
remember into something CI notices.

Note that meeting a re-open condition in `research/gpu-verdict.md` §5 does **not** by itself fire
this trigger — re-running the benchmark harness on a discrete GPU is still measurement. The
trigger is *wiring*, not *measuring*.

## 7. Recommendation and phased plan

**Recommendation: keep the runtime deferral; close the two things that are cheap and real now;
make the trigger self-detecting.** Do not write a full device threat model for a crate nothing
calls — it would be modelling a system that does not exist, and it would age badly against
whatever hardware actually motivates the re-open.

Ordered, each a future bead (P1–P4 are implementation and are deliberately not done in this
design record):

| # | work | why now | surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Add `wgpu naga wgpu-core wgpu-hal` to `FORBIDDEN` in `scripts/wasm-deps-guard.sh` | Converts §4.2's three prose assertions into the enforced gate they claim to be; one line | `scripts/` |
| P2 | Pin "nothing depends on `sparq-gpu`" as a gate, mirroring `scripts/fedclient-boundary-guard.sh`'s `cargo tree -i` inversion | Makes §6 exit-condition 1 self-detecting instead of remembered | `scripts/` |
| P3 | Close the two latent preconditions in-crate: promote `upload_table`'s `debug_assert!` to a real check that also requires a free slot; reject out-of-range GROUP BY keys with a typed error; add differential tests that feed each malformed input deliberately | §4.3 leaves the crate's central CPU==GPU equivalence claim unverified exactly where the two sides disagree | `crates/sparq-gpu` |
| P4 | Bound the WGSL probe walk at `mask + 1` iterations and give `run()` a bounded poll | §4.4 — a malformed table must not be able to wedge a device | `crates/sparq-gpu` |
| P5 | **ON EXIT ONLY** — the full model: §5.1–5.6 | Blocked on the trigger; premature otherwise | `research/` |
| P6 | State `sparq-gpu`'s exclusion explicitly in `SECURITY.md` / `ASSURANCE.md`, and re-decide §4.1's accepted cost | Neither file mentions the crate today, so "not covered" reads as an omission rather than a decision | governance docs |

P1 and P2 are the highest value per unit of work in this list: they are small, they need no
device, and they are what stop this deferral from silently expiring.

## 8. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Is §4.1's cost accepted?** 40 unvetted cargo-vet exemptions (~8% of the repo's total) exist
   solely for a parked prototype. The alternatives — moving `sparq-gpu` out of the workspace, or
   actually vetting the GPU closure — both cost more than they obviously return. This record
   recommends accepting and recording it; that is a maintainer call, not a research finding.
2. **Should P3/P4 harden a parked crate at all?** There is a defensible position that a
   measurement instrument with no external callers should stay exactly as measured, and that
   hardening it is work spent on a code path that may never ship. The counter-argument is that
   §4.3 is not hardening — it is a gap in the crate's *own* correctness claim, which is the one
   thing the crate exists to establish.
3. **Does the browser/WebGPU path (§5.5) get its own bead now?** It is the re-open condition most
   likely to fire, and it is the one that collides with §4.2.

## 9. What this record does NOT claim

- It does **not** assess any runtime, driver, residency, side-channel, or multi-tenancy property.
  Those are §5, deferred, unassessed.
- It does **not** clear `sparq-gpu` for production use, and nothing here should be read as
  softening the PARK verdict in [`research/gpu-verdict.md`](gpu-verdict.md).
- The counts in §4.1 were computed from the checked-in `Cargo.lock` and
  `supply-chain/config.toml` at the commit this record was written against; they will drift as
  the lockfile moves, and they are structural facts, not measurements.
